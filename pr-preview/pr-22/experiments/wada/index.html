What am I looking at?

A ray leaves each pixel and reflects between four unit spheres. Their centers form a regular tetrahedron. At spacing 1 the spheres touch; increasing spacing opens extra escape routes.

At a hit point, the unit normal is n = (p − c)/r. The next direction is d′ = d − 2(d·n)n. This simple rule, repeated, creates the nested imagery.

Escape basins color the face through which a ray leaves the tetrahedron: red, green, blue, or the dark entrance. Reflection depth shows the number of hits. Magenta marks rays unresolved at the bounce limit; it is not another basin.

The Wada property means every boundary point of one basin also borders all the others. This finite image illustrates chaotic scattering; it does not prove that property, especially after changing the spacing.

Radiant mirrors add colored surface lighting at every hit and attenuate later reflections. These are artistic lighting choices, not a physically calibrated light simulation. Pause and compare modes at the same camera position.

Try 4 versus 48 reflections; then move the aim by a tiny amount. Fine structures may alias at pixel scale. Native recording is real-time WebM, not lossless or guaranteed constant-frame-rate.
