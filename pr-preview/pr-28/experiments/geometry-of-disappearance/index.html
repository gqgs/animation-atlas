The geometry behind the image

Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao (also called Hellinger–Kantorovich) combines movement with local creation and destruction of mass. The continuity equation gains a reaction term:

∂ₜρ + ∇·(ρv) = ρα
action = ∫ ρ (|v|² + κ² α²/4)

In this convention, a pair of atoms can travel only when their separation is less than πκ/2. Beyond that, the shortest route is extinction here and creation there. At the cutoff, shortest paths need not be unique.

The atom preset uses the exact cone formula. For shapes, 144 weighted atoms per endpoint are coupled by a log-domain entropy-transport solver, then interpolated on the cone. Entropic regularization ε = 0.035 makes this a numerical approximation, not an exact continuum geodesic. No endpoint pairing is hand-authored.

For a branch with endpoint masses A, B, angle θ = distance/κ, and phase t:

rₜ² = (1−t)² A + t² B
+ 2t(1−t)√(AB) cos θ

Position advances by the angle of (1−t)√A + t√B exp(iθ), divided by θ. Disconnected mass stays fixed and decays as (1−t)², or grows as t². Pure reaction uses that same quadratic law at all source and destination points; it is not an ordinary linear opacity crossfade.

Glowing kernels and color are artistic rendering. Kernel width is fixed; brightness carries mass with no per-frame normalization. The graph reports mathematical mass, independent of display glow. Visual density can differ from total mass when branches overlap.
