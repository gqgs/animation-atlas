What this experiment actually measures

The full field is a stack extending into depth. One IFS cluster is shallow in Z. Check the side view before interpreting a head-on resemblance.

Protein references use actual protein heavy-atom coordinates, not a generated illustration. Both objects are centered and uniformly scaled to RMS radius 1. No stretching, nonrigid warping, chemical inference, or target-specific fitting is performed.

Simple controls ask whether a match is more specific than “roughly a ring, shell, ball, or tube.” A control winning is informative, not a failure.

Distance definition and limitations

Each object is described by its three principal RMS radii, its radial-distance quantiles, and its pairwise-distance quantiles. The distance is the unweighted mean of the three mean absolute differences:

D = (D_axes + D_radial + D_pairwise) / 3

Principal radii use all points. Radial and pairwise quantiles use seeded Monte Carlo samples (8,192 radii; 32,768 pairs; 128 quantiles). These descriptors are rotation- and translation-invariant, and size is deliberately normalized away. They are coarse and non-unique: different shapes, mirror images, connectivities, and topologies can receive similar scores. Sampling error remains. The score is uncalibrated; rankings are limited to this tiny reference set.

Source reconstruction and scope

Particle equations and random-number consumption follow banach_spiral_world.html, recovered from the original source. Defaults here use the screenshot’s ρ = 0.49, twist = 0.05, density = 1.2; assumed original seed = 1729. Layers are frozen at t = 0 using the source rotation law. This is not recovery of the exact recorded frame: its seed and absolute animation time are unknown.

Only the IFS particle positions are scored. Decorative recursive spiral lines, background stars, torus shells, fixed-point markers, glow, and the macro-helical guide tubes are excluded. The recursion slider in the original affects the decorative spiral lines, not the particle IFS. Source scopes are fixed in advance: all 12 layers, first layer, or its first arm.
